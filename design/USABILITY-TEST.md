# Usability test pack — Mu Katale

Run this with **three participants who are not friends who know the project and not classmates**.
Three is the number the brief asks for and it is enough: with a task list this short, a fourth
person repeats findings rather than adding them.

Everything you need is in this file. Print or copy the sheets in §4 before the session; do not
improvise the task wording, because the wording is what makes the three runs comparable.

---

## 1. Finding participants

Where to look, in order of preference:

- **People who actually buy produce for a household or an institution** — a school bursar, a church
  cooking group member, a market trader who sells to schools, a neighbour who does the weekly shop.
  Task 1 and 2 are Sarah's job, so a person who has done any version of it will tell you more.
- **Adults outside your course**: staff room, departmental secretaries, a hall canteen, your
  landlord, a cousin's parent, a church or mosque member, a sports club.
- **Not** classmates, not housemates, not anyone who has seen you build this. They already know
  where the buttons are, which is exactly the thing being measured.

Screen them in one message before booking:

> Quick check before we arrange a time: do you use a smartphone for shopping, banking or ordering
> (any app, any shop)? … Have you ever bought fresh produce by phone or WhatsApp? … Do you know
> anyone in CSC3119 or have you seen this project before?

Any "no" to the first two is a poor fit. Any "yes" to the third disqualifies them for the brief.

Offer something for their half hour — transport money or a bunch of bananas from the actual market
works better than thanks. Book 30 minutes, in a place where you can both talk and they can hold a
phone comfortably.

## 2. What you are testing (say nothing of this to the participant)

| Question | Where the answer comes from |
| --- | --- |
| Can a stranger assemble the right basket without being told where things are? | Task 1: taps before the first correct product opens |
| Does the delivery cascade feel like help or like a form that will not finish? | Task 1: time on WF-08, and whether they look for a free-text box |
| Do "shipped" and "paid" answer the two questions a bursar asks, on one screen? | Task 2: whether they find the money state without prompting |
| Is it clear that the phone number is the sign-in key? | Task 3: whether they change it confidently or fear locking themselves out |
| Does the app's honesty read as honesty? | Any point where they say "so it's not lying?" or ask if a courier is coming |

## 3. The session, 30 minutes

**Before (2 min).** Read this, then ask them to agree:

> Thank you for coming. I'm testing a shopping app I helped build — I'm testing **the app**, not
> you. If something is confusing, that is the app's fault and it is the thing I need to find out.
> There is no prize for finishing fast. Please think out loud as you go: say what you expect to
> happen before you tap. I'd like to take notes and record the screen; nothing leaves this course.
> Stop me any time. Do you agree to go ahead?

Write the participant's initial, not their name, at the top of the sheet. Note the device and
whether they use a shopping app regularly.

**Warm-up (2 min).** Hand them the phone on WF-01 (home). Let them scroll, tap a card, open the
drawer, and close it. No tasks yet — this removes the "where am I" noise from the real measurement.

**Tasks (18 min).** Read the instruction from §4 **once**, exactly as written. Then say nothing until
they have finished or given up. When they ask "should I do this?" answer with a question: *"what do
you expect to happen?"* When they go quiet for more than 15 seconds, prompt once: *"what are you
looking at right now?"* Never point at the screen.

**Debrief (5 min).** Ask, in this order:

1. Which of the three felt worst? (Do not ask "which was hard" — they will try to be nice.)
2. What did you expect to happen when you tapped [the thing they hesitated on]?
3. Did anything the app told you surprise you — good or bad?
4. If this were a real market, would you order from it? What would have to be different?
5. Any word on any screen you would like rewritten?

**After.** Thank them, pay them, and write up the sheet while the session is still in your head —
the same evening, before you look at the next participant's notes.

## 4. The sheets

### Task card (read aloud, once)

> **Task 1.** Sarah's school needs bananas, garlic and avocados for the week. Find all three, and
> get as far as being ready to pay — but do not actually pay.

> **Task 2.** You placed an order on Monday and the bursar wants to know whether it has left the
> market, and whether you have been charged. Show me.

> **Task 3.** The school moved its delivery point to Ntinda, and your phone number changed. Change
> what the market has on file so the next order goes to the right place.

> **Task 4 (only if they finish early).** You are the market assistant. This order has been paid in
> cash at the gate — get it on its way, then show me that the stock that left the shelves is
> recorded.

## 5. Analysing three sessions

1. Put the three sheets side by side and mark every place **two or more** people stumbled. Two out
   of three is a design fault; one out of three is a note.
2. Sort those by how much time they cost and whether the participant recovered alone. A 40-second
   detour they laughed off is not the same as a task abandoned.
3. Write the top three as changes with a screen and a reason, e.g. *"The parish dropdown reads
   'Choose sub-county first' and two people tried to tap it as a text box — make the disabled state
   unmistakable and put the reason on the label that unlocked it."*
4. Attach to the submission: the three filled sheets (initials only), the list of three findings,
   and what you would change first. A finding you acted on is worth more than ten you noted.

**Consent, in one line on the sheet:** *Notes and screen recordings are used only for this course
and kept under the participant's initials, not their name.* If a participant says no to recording,
take notes only — do not push, and do not use anything they said that you did not write down in
front of them.
